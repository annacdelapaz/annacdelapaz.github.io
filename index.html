About

I study how welfare states shape the care families can reach, from birth support and breastfeeding to food on the table in later life.

I am a PhD candidate in Sociology at Syracuse University. My research examines maternal health, doula care, and food insecurity, comparing how policy design in the United States and Norway structures access to care. I work with both qualitative interviews and national survey data, including the National Survey of Family Growth and the CDC Natality files.

Before graduate school, I worked as a WIC clinic manager and nutritionist and then as a registered dietitian. I hold an MS in Nutrition from the University of Rhode Island and an MA in Sociology from Syracuse University, and I completed doula training through DONA International.

I am currently on the academic job market.

Research

Dissertation

Doula Care in New York: Comparing Medicaid and Private-Pay Systems

I examine how payer systems shape the administrative, economic, and practice dimensions of doula work in New York State, drawing on in-depth interviews with doulas across the state. Data collection is nearly complete, and I am currently drafting chapters.

Committee University Professor Madonna Harrington Meyer (Chair), Dr. Rick Welsh, Dr. Merril Silverstein

  1. Problem

    Doula care improves birth outcomes, yet in New York access has long depended on who can pay, and maternal health risks fall hardest on Black and low-income families.

  2. Project

    I interview doulas across the state to compare Medicaid and private-pay work: how payer source shapes their pay, their care, and their paperwork.

  3. Policy

    Evidence on what it takes for New York’s Medicaid doula benefit to work for doulas and reach the families it was designed for.

Maternal health and welfare regimes

How health insurance, WIC, and national policy shape breastfeeding, doula care, and birth outcomes in the U.S. and Norway.

Birth and inequality

Racial and geographic differences in unintended home births in the United States.

Food insecurity across the life course

With Madonna Harrington Meyer, how food insecurity reshapes grandparenting and the health of low-income grandparents.

Gender, policy, and markets

The commodification of breastmilk and policy responses to the commercial sex trade.
